# Prospective five-concept descriptive protocol v6

Protocol `text-vs-labeled-diagram` v6 enrolls new learners on content `m2-draft.9` (`v9`). Learners already assigned under v1–v5 remain pinned to their original protocol and content, including assignment-only v5 records on the v8 pack. V6 does not reinterpret or migrate historical responses. Its fixed assignment seed is `20261022`; the assignment unit remains one concept per learner. The text/diagram allocation is persisted before instructional exposure and remains stable on restart.

The research question is whether a labeled diagram added to an otherwise equivalent worked explanation and retrieval-practice routine is associated with stronger delayed independent application for this learner. Both arms share the worked text, objectives, practice, feedback, and held-out checks; the diagram arm adds the labeled diagram. V9 updates the prospective content while retaining the five primary held-out family IDs, the seven-day target and days 7–14 primary window, ten-minute suggested study target, and v5 analysis rules. The minimum of 20 independent learners per arm is unchanged. Multiple concept observations from one learner are dependent and cannot satisfy that gate.

Exposure start and completion, encountered item families, condition, content and protocol versions, first answers, hints, reveals, outside-study information when supplied, and actual delayed timing are retained. Opening time is not proof of attentive study. The primary observation is the first unassisted, unfamiliar, deterministically scored held-out response within the specified window. Previously exposed families, assisted responses, outside study, invalid or ungraded answers, and responses outside the window remain in the record but are ineligible for that primary observation. Missing outcomes are reported rather than imputed. Draft or candidate answer keys remain provisional and cannot establish a verified learning effect.

Within-learner observations and arm differences are descriptive. Carryover, content differences, order, and missing return visits limit interpretation. The comparison returns `insufficient_evidence` unless its existing evidence threshold and eligibility rules are met; a favorable single result does not change instructional policy or establish causality. Synthetic test journeys verify software behavior, not actual learner retention. Qualified non-author content validation remains a separate gate and must be documented from real review evidence.
