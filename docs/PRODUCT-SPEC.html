Aesop Learning Lab — Complete Owner Product & Engineering Contract

Version: 3.0 · Created: October 29, 2026 · Designation: strategic product direction and staged engineering requirements; not a modification to existing experiments, source code or assigned worker authority.

Core proposition: A personal learning laboratory that teaches difficult concepts, measures independent understanding and delayed transfer, and cautiously investigates which instructional methods help a given learner on a given type of material. Teach effectively, measure honestly, adapt cautiously, never confuse an engaging lesson with demonstrated learning.

Existing canonical contracts take precedence for active experiments: project brief, module contracts, prospective v8 protocol, historical pilot report, team guide, real learner runbook and delivery board. This new vision does NOT authorize modifying frozen seeds, cohort assignments, content, analyses, checkpoints or scientific conclusions. Resolve discrepancies through prospective versioning and the existing review process.

Market-velocity risk: High. Well-funded platforms can add comparison dashboards, adaptive representations and diagnostic explanations. This risk is about the market moving before differentiation is proven, not how quickly our team can write code. Recheck official releases before each expansion; don't claim any rival lacks a feature without verifying it. The candidate opening is a user-visible, topic-specific learning-method laboratory with unusually transparent evidence and humility about causation.

Target audience: independent adult learners beginning programming and computer science, especially those who prefer to understand and apply concepts instead of memorizing familiar examples. Begin with Python functions and recursion. Personal use first, then research-quality external pilot; only then broaden content or consider paid distribution.

2. Complete learner journey

  1. Choose a bounded concept: Explicit objective, prerequisites and learner-adjustable pacing; do not secretly infer broad cognitive traits.
  2. Instruction: Reviewed explanation and worked example, optionally a labeled diagram or other predeclared condition.
  3. Independent attempt: Present a structurally fresh question; save the initial answer and optional confidence before any hint or feedback.
  4. Help when needed: Progressive nudge → targeted hint → worked solution. Retain first-answer status and assistance history.
  5. Explain: Show validated answer reasoning and why plausible alternatives or distractors fail; route misconceptions to suitable prerequisite practice.
  6. Apply: Ask a different question, not just repeat the demonstrated solution. Distinguish recognition, recall, explanation, application and transfer.
  7. Delayed check: Schedule real next-day/one-week follow-ups with unseen question families and actual timestamps. Do not count synthetic clock manipulation as actual learning.
  8. Recommend: Explain the next step and its evidence; permit a learner override, preserving what was recommended versus chosen.

Correctness and effort: first unaided, assisted, ungraded and later unfamiliar responses are distinct. Never collapse into an unexplained mastery percentage. Do not call a draft-answer-key match "mastery" or authoritative correctness. For free-form explanations/code design, show an ungraded rubric or qualified review until validation permits scoring.

3. Existing implemented foundation versus future scope

Repository-reported current pilot: a local five-concept Python functions/recursion loop with persistent learner records, worked-answer-first study, hints, fresh attempts, override records, delayed checks and descriptive method-comparison logic. New learner records follow draft content v11 and prospective v8 comparison protocol according to the current rule; historical versions must remain pinned. The draft scoring keys and lesson content still require independent qualified content review. Existing tests demonstrate code/state behavior, not educational efficacy.

Future aspiration: approximately 20 related, reviewed concepts according to the existing original V1 brief, then selected CS/DS&A concepts. This aspirational scope must not be represented as current implementation. Do not replace the five-concept experimental pilot with a generic LLM tutor.

4. Two-tier adaptation and scientifically honest comparison

Tier A — Within-session assistance

Use transparent deterministic signals (errors, hint use, independent attempts, prerequisite gaps, due reviews) to recommend practice, explanations or a different fresh task. Log recommendation and optional user override. A learner preference (e.g. diagrams feel pleasant) is not proof of superior retention.

Tier B — Instructional method comparison

The first existing study compares a fixed text instruction approach against text plus labeled diagram under a prospectively frozen protocol. Possible future separately designed comparisons: worked-example-first vs initial prediction; diagram vs no diagram on selected concept; scaffold fading; interleaving vs blocked practice. These are separate experimental questions, never silently added to the active v8 assignment.

v8 rules to preserve: concept/learner assignment uses predeclared mappings and seed 20261021; new learners use v11 draft; earlier learners remain pinned. Existing v8 states at least 20 independent learners per arm, not 20 observations; same learner across concepts is correlated, not independent participants. Apply the defined first independent unfamiliar delayed response in the appropriate window, with consistent exposure target and outside-study handling. Structured report of outside study up to the first held-out response may contaminate eligibility. Distinguish early, late, previously seen, assisted, missing, ungraded and invalid trials. Maintain insufficient_evidence unless valid eligible outcomes and independently reviewed content support an actual finding.

Never: infer method victory from single-learner experience, synthetic tests, draft keys, raw engagement, an unreviewed question, casual preference, or uncontrolled differences in difficulty/exposure. Do not repeatedly replan hypotheses after reading outcomes without marking exploration. Comparative studies should use a competent, fixed active-learning baseline with retrieval, feedback and spacing, not a weak reading-only strawman.

Evidence levels and communication

LevelPermitted conclusion
ObservationThe learner did X under Y conditions; explanation unknown.

5. Reviewed content and assessment contracts

Each versioned concept pack must specify: concept ID and objective, prerequisites, source/citation, authored lesson, permitted representations, one or more worked solutions, diverse fresh practice families, progressive hints, scored candidate answer keys, rationale, misconception/distractor explanations, independent follow-up families, answer rubric, timing policy, content version, and author/reviewer status. Require independent qualified subject-matter validation before representing candidate answers as verified.

Problem-family safeguards: held-out items must be structurally unlike instructed/practiced examples; track every family/variant the learner saw. A hint-assisted or previously exposed family must not become new independent transfer evidence. All common incorrect reasoning paths should produce distinguishable wrong outputs and correct rubric expectations. Example: the current v11 recursion family correction involving converging meet(low, high) must retain its frozen intent unless a new prospective review changes it.

Scoring: bounded multiple choice, deterministic code traces and short closed-form outcomes may be scored by reviewed deterministic validators. Do not execute arbitrary learner code in initial release. Preserve explanations as ungraded or human/rubric reviewed when no reliable objective grader exists.

Use the test commands and review lanes already defined in the agent team guide. A full test pass, fast simulation, or large answer count does not prove learning gains. Before shipping new content, obtain a qualified human reviewer and preserve their actual receipt; don't fabricate signoff.

8. Development sequence

  1. P0: finish existing five-concept pilot. Review content and candidate keys independently, verify all state/round-trip/override contracts, check genuinely elapsed one-day/seven-day observations without upgrading the results to causal proof.
  2. P1: polished daily-driver lesson experience with clear feedback, history, accessibility, content provenance, learner autonomy and reliable recovery.
  3. P2: prospective controlled method comparison with defined independent participants, equal exposure, validated held-out assessment families, corrected uncertainty and honest reporting.
  4. P3: expand to ~20 validated related concepts, then selected CS, recursion, debugging, binary search and other DS&A applications as content quality permits.
  5. P4: external learner test, baseline competition, retention/effectiveness and commercial feasibility. Do not expand broadly without positive evidence.

9. Go/no-go and non-goals

Go: useful lessons, reviewed material, sustained learning and a credible incremental value above a competent fixed routine. Revise: learner engagement rises but transfer/delayed independent performance does not. Hold: unreviewed answers dominate, data are inadequate, or the instructional effect fails to replicate.

Non-goals: general chatbot tutor, all-subject curriculum, unsupported mastery scores, permanent "visual/auditory learner" labels, inferential psychological traits, model training, unbounded cloud cost, replacing the frozen experimental protocol, automatic medical/education claims, or adding features just to match every competitor.

Governance: Work must follow this repository's existing author/reviewer and owner-decision rules, not a presumed universal Director-Orchestrator workflow. No change to real learners, approvals, grades, publication or deployments without authorized source-based process. Docs remain standalone HTML with compatible light/dark rendering.
