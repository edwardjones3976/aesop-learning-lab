Aesop continuation: one-week measurement

Selection

At the start of this continuation, branch codex/foreman-v1 was clean at ade999054a8b79aaeaac96b62798a87399de032a. The working v2 preview had a one-day check only. The project brief requires configurable one-day and one-week checks. This continuation's objective is an end-to-end one-week check with a distinct held-out item, clock-controlled tests, and separate outcome reporting.

Using the mission weights (learning objective 30%, verified delivery 25%, measurement 20%, architecture 15%, progress per dollar 10%), this objective was favored over a probabilistic learner model and a broader experiment. The one-week check fills a measurable workflow gap with bounded code and no runtime AI or paid research call. These are qualitative priority judgments, not empirical effect estimates. The one-week interval is a pilot default from the brief, not an optimal interval established for these Python concepts.

Design and scientific boundary

The v3 pack adds one seven-day trace item per concept, each with a family distinct from the one-day item. All 22 items and five concepts are versioned v3. Existing v1 and v2 packs remain unchanged and their learner histories remain pinned. The new pack is draft, has candidate keys, and has no independent review receipts. The v3 policy (pilot-v2) uses a saved lesson time, observed attempts, and item-specific delay. A completed day-one check opens the week-one check even when day-one was incorrect or assisted. Conditioning week-one eligibility on day-one success would systematically omit weak outcomes.

Separate interval statuses distinguish an observed day-one answer from missing week-one evidence. Assisted answers never count as independent success. Synthetic clock progression verifies software behavior, not learning effectiveness. There is no claim of mastery, human retention improvement, or causal benefit.

The choice to measure at a week is consistent with research using delayed tests to distinguish immediate performance from later retention. For example, Karpicke and Roediger's retrieval experiment and the retrieval versus study analysis of initially retrievable material motivate delayed observation. Neither validates the effect of this app's new items or policy; that requires reviewed content and learner data.
