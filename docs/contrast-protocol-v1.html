Contrast protocol v1

Frozen specification, 2026-10-21; policy ID contrast-order-v1-policy-1. Decision source: aesop-declutter-plan/realign-decision.html. This document specifies future implementation; it does not deploy a contrast or claim that the simulation gate has passed. Independent acceptance by Claude and Cursor and Grok's protocol audit remain required.

1. Scope and eligible unit

Run one two-arm contrast per experiment per learner. Contrast ID: order-v1; task scope: delayed-recall; topic: the enrolled pack's declared topic. Every finding, posterior snapshot and learner-facing result carries that contrast ID, topic and task scope, plus learner, experiment and pack/version identifiers. Findings concern these items and this delay, not a stable learner trait or general educational effectiveness.

This is a separate personal product layer. The group descriptive protocol, its allocation, classification and reports are unchanged. Do not pool learners or import historical outcomes into this experiment. Replace Thompson allocation only in this contrast's judging path.

The unit is one fresh, independently taught micro-unit with one completed exposure and one decisive first response to its day-7 check, weight exactly 1. Multiple practice items, answers or checks sharing an exposure do not create extra units. Use the engine's inclusive window: seven through fourteen elapsed days after completion. A first response outside that window is retained but excluded; later responses cannot replace it. Only unassisted, uncontaminated, in-window first responses contribute. Missing checks are not failures.

Preserve the engine's contamination rules: reported outside study some, prior familiarity, or recorded re-exposure excludes the unit; none and unknown retain the existing treatment. Retain declarations and exclusion reasons. Completed-unit replay remains refused. A known contaminated unit is excluded even if its answer preceded the recorded re-exposure. Practice and day-one results never enter this estimator. Matching uses the engine's existing draft-key scoring; preserve the key version and provisional status. No pooling of reviewed and draft targets or changed keys within a frozen run.

Each fresh item has both authored versions, but each learner sees only its assigned version. Reorder the same components; change no wording, examples, prompts, hints, feedback, study budget or assessment. The day-7 prompt and accepted key are identical across arms and held out from teaching. Require max(arm word counts) / min(arm word counts) ≤ 1.05 per paired unit, counting all learner-visible lesson text, prompts and feedback with the same whitespace-token rule. Record both counts. The 5% allowance does not authorize additional instructional content. Freeze the pack, recipes, checks and keys before enrollment; changes require a new experiment/version.

3. Allocation and time

Use 1:1 permuted blocks of four consecutive units in the engine's existing seeded per-learner shuffled order (#65): exactly two BASELINE and two VARIANT assignments in each full block. These are allocation blocks, distinct from pack difficulty labels. A partial final block retains its preassigned prefix; do not rebalance based on outcomes or completion. No adaptive or outcome-dependent allocation is allowed while judging.

For replay without implementation-specific random shuffles, define each block seed as the lowercase SHA-256 hex digest of UTF-8 compact JSON [learner_id,pack_id,"order-v1",block_index], using zero-based block indices, no spaces, and JSON Unicode escaping. For positions 0–3, hash the ASCII string block_seed + ":" + position; sort ascending by digest, breaking ties by position. Assign BASELINE to the first two ranked positions and VARIANT to the other two. Store the full block seed and all four positions/arms atomically before any item in that block is exposed. Persist the unit-order mapping and policy version before first exposure, including unused final-block slots. Restart reuses these records. Outcome updates never alter them. A different experiment cannot reuse taught items as fresh units.

Day zero is the learner's first actual exposure in this experiment, an immutable aware timestamp. Scheduled cutoffs are exactly 14, 21 and 28 elapsed 24-hour days later, independent of local midnight or visits. Evaluate only these cutoffs, in order, once each; day 28's look is also the final call, not an extra fourth look. Use responses received at or before the cutoff, with eligibility known at that cutoff; persist the input snapshot. If evaluation runs late, reconstruct that snapshot rather than use later responses. Do not backdate findings using information learned later. Log later contamination corrections beside the original decision and flag the affected finding; do not silently rewrite it or resume a stopped contrast.

4. Simulation pass gate (pre-registered)

500 synthetic learners per scenario (10 seeds × 50), pooled, at 3 units a day. Rates are judged by one-sided 95% Wilson bounds, so a rate sitting exactly on a threshold does not pass. Outcomes come from a recall model that is independent of the estimator.

ScenarioRequirement
No difference, and a 3-point gapAny "better" or "promising" call: upper bound ≤ 5%. At least 50% reach "no meaningful difference" by day 28. "No difference" or "insufficient": at least 90%.
10-point gapThe true arm is ranked first in at least 80% by day 28, and every seed is at least 70%. The worse arm is called superior in ≤ 5%. The median decision day is ≤ 24.
20-point gapThe correct superiority call is made in at least 90% by day 21.
10-point gap with 20% missed days, and with 10% dropoutAt least 70% correct by day 28, with the same 5% bound on wrong calls.
AI learner (Antigravity) zero-shot pretest on the invented packFree-recall accuracy ≤ 5% before any study, logged item by item.

If the gate fails, Codex may revise only the pre-registered margin or the interim days, once. The run is then repeated once on a fresh seed set. If it fails again, the admins redesign.

5. Estimator, thresholds and stopping

For each arm independently, with s eligible draft-key matches and f eligible nonmatches, use Beta(1+s,1+f). Define diff = p_variant - p_baseline. The practical equivalence region is strictly -0.05 < diff < 0.05. Compute posterior probabilities by deterministic numerical integration of the independent beta densities/CDFs, with absolute error at most 0.000001; no unseeded Monte Carlo decisions. If a numerical error interval crosses a threshold, refine integration until the comparison resolves; unresolved computation is an explicit error, never a positive decision.

This is the decision's proposed alpha-spending-style schedule: stricter early thresholds limit premature calls when evidence is sparse, while later thresholds permit a conclusion with more observations. These Bayesian cutoffs are not a proved frequentist alpha-spending guarantee. The copied simulation gate below must validate repeated-look error and recovery; the thresholds cannot be tuned after seeing gate results under its one-time revision rule.

Before any outcome decision, require at least eight eligible outcomes in each arm, including on day 28. At an eligible look apply the following precedence:

  1. If P(diff > 0.05) meets the superiority threshold, stop with repeated benefit, winner VARIANT. Symmetrically, if P(diff < -0.05) meets it, stop with repeated benefit, winner BASELINE. This label maps to the founding concept; one scheduled crossing suffices, not repeated significance tests.
  2. Otherwise, if P(|diff| < 0.05) meets the equivalence threshold, stop with no meaningful difference detected.
  3. Otherwise at day 14 or 21, continue unchanged; the snapshot records continue, not a terminal outcome.
  4. Otherwise at day 28, stop with promising if P(diff > 0) ≥ 0.80, and insufficient evidence otherwise. Promising is the preregistered directional claim for VARIANT only; a merely suggestive baseline advantage is insufficient evidence.

If either arm has fewer than eight eligible outcomes, continue at days 14/21 and stop with insufficient evidence at day 28 regardless of posterior probabilities. A terminal decision stops allocation and further judging for this contrast. Already scheduled checks may be collected as separately labeled post-stop follow-up, excluded from its frozen decision. No unscheduled looks, rolling labels, threshold updates or outcome-driven exposure changes.

6. Learner wording, preference and override

Always show the scope, eligible counts per arm, exclusions, decision day and draft-key caveat beside the result. Use “matched the draft key”; never describe results as mastery. Before a terminal result, say: “We are still collecting later checks. The next planned comparison is on day [day].”

OutcomePlain learner wording
repeated benefit“On these later checks, your answers matched the draft key more often with [winning method], beyond our five-point margin under this model. This finding applies to [topic] delayed-recall checks and may change in a new comparison.”
no meaningful difference detected“On these later checks, we found no meaningful difference within five percentage points in how often your answers matched the draft key. You can choose which order you prefer.”
promising“Trying the question first looks promising for how often your answers matched the draft key, but we have not established a meaningful benefit. You can choose your preferred order.”
insufficient evidence“We do not have enough evidence to say these orders differ meaningfully in how often your answers matched the draft key. You can choose your preferred order.”

After equivalence or either inconclusive day-28 result (promising or insufficient), the latest stated preference, if any, selects the future-study default. Without a preference, retain the strong baseline. After repeated benefit, use the winning method as the future default unless overridden. A preference never changes the posterior or counts as effectiveness evidence. Store every learner override beside the evidence with the selected method, aware timestamp and decision reference. An override during judging does not rewrite allocation: if it changes delivered content, stop participation in that frozen experiment, retain the last snapshot, and issue insufficient evidence at day 28 unless a valid earlier terminal decision exists. Do not count override-delivered items as randomized evidence.

7. Later ladder (not implemented now)

Freeze an ordered candidate roster for N methods before starting a future ladder. Compare an incumbent with the next challenger using fresh units and a separately frozen contrast ID, policy, paired content, margin, looks and evidence. The winner advances; after equivalence or an inconclusive stop the preferred method advances, with baseline/incumbent fallback if no preference. Repeat until all candidates have met the incumbent. Display the resulting procedural order with each pair's scoped evidence and preference basis. It is not proof of every pairwise relationship or a transitive total effectiveness ranking; untested pairs remain untested. Do not recycle outcomes across rounds or implement this ladder in step 1.

8. Stored evidence and implementation handoff

  • Experiment, learner and pack IDs; pack/topic/task scope and contrast ID on every finding; policy, content/recipe, check and key versions; provisional draft-key status.
  • Both arm IDs, roles and frozen recipes; paired item IDs and word counts; shuffled unit order; block index, full block seed, four-slot allocation and unit-to-slot mapping persisted before exposure.
  • First-exposure origin, assignment/exposure/completion timestamps, decisive first response and received time, score, assistance, outside-study declaration, familiarity, re-exposure and exclusions. Store the original delivered method and any deviation.
  • Frozen look schedule and exact cutoff timestamps, ROPE, all thresholds, prior, minimum eight per arm, estimator/numerical version and stopping precedence.
  • At each look: cutoff and evaluation time, immutable eligible/excluded unit snapshot, successes/failures and counts per arm, alpha/beta, posterior means and equal-tailed 95% credible intervals per arm and for diff, P(diff > 0.05), P(diff < -0.05), P(|diff| < 0.05), P(diff > 0), numerical error tolerance, and continue/terminal status.
  • Terminal outcome, winner if any, decision day (14/21/28), decision time, stopped status; post-stop data and corrections explicitly separate from the frozen finding.
  • Stated preference and its timestamp/history, selected future default and its basis; override method, timestamp and decision reference stored beside evidence.
